A quiz answer can wait a second. A ghost in a maze can't. This part puts the direct readout from [the first part](/blog/llm-choices-in-the-browser) in a place where latency is the whole game.

## The ghosts 👻

Three ghosts run the original arcade logic: Blinky chases you, Pinky aims four tiles ahead of you, and Clyde gets shy up close. The fourth one, the green 🤖 with the antenna, has no logic of its own.

As soon as it leaves a crossroads, it asks the model which way to turn at the next one. The green arrows are the model's answer, sized by probability, and a dashed **?** marks the crossroads it is still thinking about.

If the answer hasn't arrived by the time the ghost gets there, it stops with a 💭 and waits. That pause is the model's latency, right in the maze. After 2.5 seconds it gives up and falls back to the arcade rules.

## Give the ghost a brain 🧠

This page starts on the smallest model. Here a fast wrong answer beats a slow right one. Without a model, the 🤖 turns at random.

## What the ghost is asked

Every crossroads is one direct readout. The options are only the open directions, never the way back, and the question flips when you eat a power pellet:

```text title="prompt"
State:
You are a ghost in a Pac-Man maze, standing at a crossroads. Pac-Man is
10 tiles down and 7 tiles to the left from you. You want to catch him.

Question:
Which way takes you toward Pac-Man?

Allowed options:
A. Up
B. Down

Reply with exactly one option letter from: A, B.
```

The ghost asks with Pac-Man's position from when it left the last crossroads, so by the time it turns, you have moved. A faster model is a scarier ghost. With a bigger one you'll see it stop at the crossroads a lot more, and that pause is how you escape.
