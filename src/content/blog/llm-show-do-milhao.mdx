In [the first part](/blog/llm-choices-in-the-browser) we read a model's choice straight from the log-probabilities of the option letters: one forward pass, about a second, no text generated. That is fast enough to sit in a quiz show audience.

## The rules 📜

Ten questions from R$ 1,000 to R$ 1 million, with the rules of the Brazilian show:

- **🛑 Stop** and you keep what you have.
- **❌ Get it wrong** and you leave with half.
- **💥 Miss the million-real question** and you leave with nothing.

You get three lifelines: **✂️ 50:50** once, **⏭️ Skip** three times (same prize, new question), and **📊 Audience** twice.

## What the audience really is

It is the direct readout from part 1, run only on the answers still on screen. After a 50:50 it votes between two. The prompt says a contestant is asking for help and that the answer must be factually correct, and the percentages are a softmax over the letter tokens.

That makes them a confidence, not a poll, and not a calibrated one: a small model can put 98% on the wrong planet. At the end of a game, the status line tells you how many times the audience was right.
